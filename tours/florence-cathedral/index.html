Santa Maria del Fiore and the Cathedral Square

Architecture, sculpture and engineering around Florence's cathedral complex and Brunelleschi's extraordinary dome.

The heart of monumental Florence

The first stone of Santa Maria del Fiore was laid in 1296. Over the centuries the cathedral became the setting for major events in Florence's history and, with Brunelleschi's dome, one of the most recognizable monuments of the city.

The visit can begin in the Cathedral Museum, renovated and reopened in 2015, where original works created for the cathedral and the surrounding monuments are preserved. One section is devoted to Brunelleschi's dome and another reconstructs the appearance of the cathedral's early fourteenth-century façade.

From the museum the route continues through the cathedral complex and the Baptistery, famous for its mosaics. Camilla's conservation background makes it possible to look closely at how architecture, sculpture and decorative techniques were actually conceived and made.

Access to the dome climb depends on current tickets and entry arrangements; when appropriate it can be planned separately around the guided visit.
