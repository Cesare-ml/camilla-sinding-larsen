The classic Florence city tour

A walk through Florence's historic centre, from the medieval city to the monuments that became symbols of the Renaissance.

Get to know Florence on foot

Florence's historic centre is compact enough to discover on foot. Walking among narrow streets, old palaces and public squares gives you a direct sense of the city's history and of how different periods still coexist in everyday Florence.

By the end of the thirteenth century Florence had become a wealthy and powerful trading city. Many of the buildings that still define its skyline date from this period, including Santa Maria del Fiore and the civic centre around Piazza della Signoria. Brunelleschi's great dome later became one of the clearest symbols of the Renaissance city.

The route can take in Palazzo Vecchio, the exterior of the Uffizi, the medieval quarter associated with Dante and examples of the tower houses in which Florentine families once lived. The walk naturally reaches Ponte Vecchio, Florence's oldest bridge over the Arno, with its characteristic goldsmith shops.

Camilla connects the monuments with the people who built, commissioned and used them, making the city easier to read as a living whole rather than a collection of isolated sights.
